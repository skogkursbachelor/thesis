\chapter{Conclusion}\label{chap:conclusion}

The aim of this project was to develop and test a \textbf{prototype system for fully digital modeling of forestry road load-bearing capacity under varying conditions throughout the year.} Skogkurs wanted to investigate the prototype as a proof of concept and evaluate its feasibility and effectiveness as a foundation for a future operational system in the forestry industry. 

The system consists of an interactive, map-based website that visualizes the trafficability of Norwegian forestry roads using a traffic-light classification system. Based on key environmental factors such as soil moisture, frost depth, and surrounding superficial deposits, the application provides historic, current, and forecasts of road trafficability up to nine days ahead.

Although no formal user testing of the product was conducted, limiting the evaluation of usability and universal design, other forms of feedback were incorporated. Two presentations were held for forestry professionals where we gathered feedback directly. In addition, continuous collaboration with the \gls{productowner} provided important insight into real-world communication and iterative development. These activities contributed to our understanding of how to work iteratively and apply agile principles like Scrum and Kanban in a practical setting.

A particularly valuable aspect of the project was the opportunity to work with map-based technologies and geospatial data, including services and formats such as \gls{wms} and \gls{wfs}, as well as tools like OpenLayers. We also expanded our knowledge of backend development through work with \acrshort{rest}ful \acrshort{api}s and concurrent processing.

To our knowledge, this is the first solution that presents trafficability in this format, making it an innovative contribution to the field. We believe the prototype has demonstrated that the concept can improve route planning efficiency for transport managers, and should therefore be further investigated and developed.
